0 · The whole idea in 30 seconds

Imagine you ask a careful research team: “What are the best IT companies to invest in, in India?” Here's what happens behind the scenes:

  1. A librarian figures out which companies you might mean and makes a list.
  2. A calculator robot downloads free public numbers (profits, debt, price trends) and scores each company — pure math, no opinions — plus a “how trustworthy is this data?” sticker.
  3. A panel of AI analysts reads those scores and writes short, evidence-based opinions. They cannot make up numbers.
  4. Two AIs debate — one optimistic “Bull,” one cautious “Bear.”
  5. A risk & tax checker adds rules specific to you (e.g. local capital-gains tax, or “keep it low-risk for a student”).
  6. A manager combines everything into a final ranked list and writes you a tidy report.
💡
The one analogy to remember

The robot does the maths; the AI only does the talking. Numbers always come from real data and formulas — the AI is never allowed to invent a figure. That's what makes the output more trustworthy than asking a chatbot to “just pick stocks.”

1 · What this project actually is

The Finance Research Agent (FRA) is a command-line program that ranks the “best” stocks to research for a theme (like “best IT stocks in India”) or a single company. It blends deterministic financial math (reproducible, never guesses) with AI language models (which explain and add context). The result is a written research report tailored to one of two saved profiles: an Indian adult investor, or a German student investor.

💬
In plain English: “deterministic”

Deterministic just means “the same inputs always give the same answer — no randomness, no guessing.” Run it twice with the same data and you get identical numbers. FRA even prints a short fingerprint (an input_hash) so you can prove it.

💻CLI (command-line interface)

A program you drive by typing a command instead of clicking buttons.

Like texting an order to the kitchen instead of pointing at a menu.

🏷️Ticker

A stock's short code, e.g. INFY.NS (Infosys) or SAP.DE (SAP).

Like a phone contact's short name.

🌎Universe

The full pool of candidate companies before the list is narrowed down.

The whole bookshelf, before you pick a few books.

🧑‍💼Profile

A saved set of preferences (country, currency, tax, risk appetite).

Your saved coffee order the barista already knows.

📊Factor

A measurable quality of a company. FRA uses five: Quality, Value, Momentum, Financial Health, Earnings Quality.

Like grading a car on safety, mileage, price, looks and reliability.

🧮Composite score

One number from 0 to 1 that blends the five factors using your chosen weights. Higher ranks better.

A single star-rating made from several sub-scores.

📏Percentile rank

“Better than X% of the others” — scored against peers, not an absolute bar.

A class rank, not a fixed exam pass-mark.

🤖LLM (large language model)

An AI that reads and writes text (GPT, Claude, or a local Llama). Here it plays “analyst.”

A very well-read intern who can summarise, but mustn't invent figures.

🛡️Data health card

A traffic-light (OK / WARN / CRITICAL) showing how complete and trustworthy this run's data was.

The freshness sticker on a carton of milk.

📊Coverage

How much of the expected data actually showed up. Low coverage → the score is nudged toward neutral.

A half-filled form: you trust its conclusions less.

🧬Snapshot

A tidy bundle of one company's numbers (price, margins, debt, momentum…).

A nutrition label for a stock.

⚖️Bull vs Bear

Two AIs argue the optimistic (Bull) vs cautious (Bear) case so you see both sides.

A friendly courtroom: prosecution and defence.

💬
In plain English: “agent,” “pipeline,” “orchestrator”

An agent is just one worker that does a single job. A pipeline is the conveyor belt those workers stand along. The orchestrator is the floor manager who runs them in the right order and passes the work down the line.

ℹ️
A note on “MCP”

You may see MCP (Model Context Protocol) mentioned in the code — a standard way for AI tools to call outside data. It is not wired into FRA today; the shipped version talks to free sources (yfinance / Stooq) directly. It's noted only as a possible future swap.

The big picture

3 · How the pieces fit together

FRA is a chain of helpers. Your typed question enters on the left and flows through each stage, getting richer at every step, until it becomes a report. A single shared “clipboard” (called the AgentState) travels the whole way, collecting results.

Three layers, in plain terms

1 · Get data

Fetch numbers & news from free sources and cache them on disk. (src/data/)

2 · Think

Deterministic math scores companies; AI interprets and debates. (src/factors/, src/agents/)

3 · Write it down

Produce the Markdown / Excel report and log the run. (src/report/, src/memory/)

✓
The whole thing in one line (from the README)

Universe → DataProvider → FactorEngine → Analysts → Bull/Bear Debate → Risk+Profile → Manager → Report

The journey, step by step

4 · The 9 stages, start to finish

The orchestrator runs the stages below in order. After the calculator robot finishes, there's one fork in the road — if you asked for no AI (or nothing made the shortlist), it takes a shortcut straight to the manager.

⚠
The one fork in the road: “Use AI?”

Right after Stage 2, FRA asks should_run_llm. If you passed --no-llm, or nothing made the shortlist, it skips Stages 3–4 and jumps straight to the manager using math only — then risk, report, memory. You still get a complete report.

0

You run a commandYou

You type a request in the terminal — which profile to use, what to research, how many picks you want.

Handled by research() in src/cli.py; it packs everything into a shared AgentState.

1

The librarian builds the candidate listNo AI

It works out whether you typed one company or a theme (“best banks in India”), spots the sector from keywords, and pulls the matching companies. Live lists first, built-in lists as backup. No AI — just rules.

src/agents/universe.py → produces candidate_tickers.

2

The calculator robot fetches data & scores everyoneNo AI

The heart of the whole thing. It downloads a snapshot for each company (cross-checking the price against a second free source), builds the data health card, then scores every company on the five factors and blends them into one composite score. Companies with thin data get pulled toward neutral so a lucky gap can't fake a high score.

src/agents/quant.py + the factor engine in src/factors/engine.py → shortlist, data_health, a reproducible input_hash.

3

Four AI analysts explain the numbersAI

Now the AI joins — but on a tight leash. Four analysts (fundamentals, technical, news, macro) read the scores and write short, plain opinions. Each is forbidden from inventing numbers and must cite the metric behind every claim. No AI available? Each one falls back to a simple math rule.

src/agents/fundamentals.py, technical.py, news_sentiment.py, macro.py → analyst_signals.

4

Bull vs Bear hold a short debateAI

Two AI personas — an optimist and a sceptic — argue the 2–3 most interesting names, so the report shows both the upside and the worries.

src/agents/researchers.py → debate turns (repeat with --rounds).

5

The risk & tax checker adds your rulesNo AI

Applies your profile rules: a cap on how much any single name can take, volatility filters, ETF preference, and tax notes (Indian capital-gains, or German Abgeltungssteuer) copied straight from your settings file.

src/agents/risk_profile.py → risk_notes, tax_notes.

6

The manager makes the final ranked picksMixed

Combines the scores, the analyst notes and the debate into a final ranked list. Each pick gets a 2–3 sentence reason (citing real metrics), key risks, a suggested holding time and an estimated after-tax return. Forgotten a shortlisted name? It's quietly backfilled.

src/agents/manager.py (run / run_quant_only) → picks.

7

The report writer saves your documentOutput

Turns everything into a clean Markdown report (and, unless you opt out, a multi-sheet Excel workbook) saved into reports/.

src/report/generator.py → report_path (+ excel_path).

8

The memory log remembers the runOutput

Appends a compact record of the run so the history command can list it later.

src/memory/store.py → a line in .fra_memory/index.jsonl.

5 · Inputs & outputs

What you provide

OptionWhat it means
--profile (required)india_adult or germany_student
--target (required)a ticker (SAP.DE) or a theme (“best IT stocks in India”)
--tophow many picks to return (default 10)
--universe / --domainnarrow the candidate pool

--no-excelskip the Excel workbook
--as-of YYYY-MM-DDevaluate as of a past date

What you get back

  • A Markdown report in reports/ and (optionally) an Excel workbook.
  • A console summary: a “Top picks” table and a data-health line.
  • A history entry you can later list with python -m src.cli history.
📄
What's inside the report (a real example)

A header (target, universe, top-N, fingerprint hash) and the disclaimer; a data health card (e.g. OK, 6/6 tickers fetched, 82% coverage); factor warnings; the final picks (each with score, coverage, confidence, suggested horizon, after-tax estimate, a short thesis, risks and tax notes); a factor-breakdown table; the analyst signals; the Bull-vs-Bear transcript; risk & tax notes; and a methodology section.

6 · Configuration & setup

Install & run (Windows / PowerShell)

# set up a clean environment and install dependencies
python -m venv .venv && .venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
copy .env.example .env

# (optional) a local, free AI:
ollama pull llama3.1:8b
ollama serve

# run a research pass:
python -m src.cli research --profile india_adult --target "best IT stocks in India" --top 10

# offline / deterministic (no AI):
python -m src.cli research --profile india_adult --target "best banks in India" --top 10 --no-llm

✓
No API key? It still runs.

If no AI is reachable, FRA quietly swaps in a deterministic stub and every analyst falls back to its math rule. You still get a valid, factor-based report — just with sparser AI prose.

⚙️
In plain English: “config-as-data”

The two profile files in config/profiles/ hold the factor weights, tax rules, risk limits and currency. So most behaviour can be changed by editing a settings file, with no Python edits: india_adult.yaml (INR, NIFTY, momentum-tilted) and germany_student.yaml (EUR, DAX/MDAX, lower-risk).

One real request, traced

7 · A worked example

Running research --profile india_adult --target "best IT companies in India" --top 10:

  1. Front desk loads the India profile, builds the clipboard, prints a banner.
  2. Librarian spots “IT” → Information Technology, and lists TCS, Infosys, Tech Mahindra, Wipro, HCLTech, LTIMindtree.
  3. Calculator fetches each snapshot (data health: OK, 6/6 fetched, 82% coverage) and scores them; fingerprint 1aceed39273729da.
  4. Analysts weigh in — e.g. TCS looks strong on fundamentals (ROE 48.4%) but weak on momentum (−31.9%); every claim cites a real number.
  5. Debate: Bull says “world-class franchises after a reset”; Bear says “the cycle is still rolling over.”
  6. Risk + tax adds a 10% per-name cap and Indian capital-gains notes.
  7. Manager ranks them (TCS #1, Infosys #2…), each with a thesis and after-tax estimate. One name had 0% data, so it's flagged low-confidence — graceful degradation in action.
  8. Report + memory save the file and log the run.
